# Cycle 1 goal

> Honestly, right now my bookmarks are a mess. I dump links in my browser and I can never find anything again, so this is really about being able to *find* and *revisit* things later, not just hoard them.
>
> A few things I'd want to feel different:
>
> When I save a link, I don't want to have to type out what it is — I'd like the app to just grab the page's title and a short description and maybe its little icon on its own, so my list is actually readable at a glance instead of a wall of raw web addresses. I should be able to fix up that title or description if it grabbed something ugly.
>
> The big one is organizing and finding. I'd want to put labels on things — like "recipes" or "work" — and then click a label to see just those. And a proper search box that looks through everything I've saved, so if I half-remember a word I can still dig the link back out.
>
> I'd also love a "read this later" pile that I can look at on its own, and a way to tuck old stuff out of sight without actually throwing it away — because today my only options are keep it cluttering everything up or delete it forever.
>
> And a small thing that drives me nuts today: if I try to save something I already saved, I don't want a second copy — I'd expect it to just take me to the one I already have.
>
> That's the heart of it for me.

Confirmed direction:

> Yes, that's exactly the right direction. That core journey — save a link, have it turn into something readable on its own, then actually find it and reopen it later — is the thing I care about most, so starting there makes sense to me.
>
> The one thing I want to make sure isn't lost as a detail: when I reopen something, tapping the item in my list should take me straight to the actual website, not just to some info page about the link. That "find it and go" part is the whole point.
>
> The rest — labels, search, read-later, archiving, editing, and the no-duplicates behavior — I'm glad those are all on your radar as part of the same goal. I'm happy to see them come in as you build it out. Go ahead.

> A web app, please. Something I open in my browser on my computer — that's where I do most of my saving and reading anyway. It's really just for me, so I don't need anything fancy with other people's accounts or logins; I just want to be able to get to my own collection when I sit down at my desk.
