# Business goal

## Goal statement (in the client's words)

> "I want to stop losing track of things I've saved. When I vaguely remember
> saving something, I want to type in whatever I can recall and have it come
> back up — instead of it vanishing into a black hole across my laptop and
> phone. And I want a place to park things I mean to read but haven't gotten to
> yet, so they don't get buried."

Confirmed by client (cycle 1).

## Core of the goal

- The heart of this is **finding things back**, not merely storing them.
- **Saving must stay effortless** — it is a means, so that the client keeps
  doing it. The payoff is retrieval.
- Retrieval works from **partial memory**: a stray word from the title, or a
  vague sense of what it was about.
- The client wants a place to **park things to read later** so they don't get
  buried.

## Scope clarifications

- "One tidy place, all in one home" — the client wants everything to live
  together instead of being scattered.
- The laptop/phone scattering is a **symptom of the current mess, not a
  requested feature**. Cross-device sync is explicitly out of scope.
- The client is happy to go to that one place to save and search.
- For now this is **single-user (just the client)**. No other users considered.

## Interface form

- **Web app — a page opened in the browser**, "like visiting a website."
- Confirmed by client (cycle 1).
- Client is non-technical: explicitly *not* a terminal tool, and does not want
  to install a program. "Just a page I go to."
- Prototyping method (per process): HTML-based interactive prototype.

## Notes

- Verbatim client emphasis: "one tidy place, and getting back what I've saved.
  That's the win."
