# Cycle 1 goal

Status: confirmed

## Goal statement

“Basically, saving should be effortless and finding things again should be reliable.”

The app should turn a pasted link into a meaningful saved item with the page's title and details, make saved items reliably searchable and organizable through tags, sorting, filtering, and eventually reusable saved searches, and distinguish between items to read later, items tucked away from the everyday list without deletion, and items permanently deleted. Saved items should remain available and understandable even if the original page changes or disappears. It should also help prevent piles of duplicates and links the client no longer cares about.

The interface will be a web app used by one person in a desktop browser. It does not need separate user accounts or sign-in.

## Client wording

> Right now my bookmarks are scattered and messy. I save a link and then can't find it again — there's no real way to search through them or organize them, and I end up with piles of duplicates and links I've long stopped caring about. Half the time all I've got saved is a raw web address with no idea what the page even was.
>
> What I want this app to feel like: I paste in a link and it just *works* — it grabs the page's title and details for me so I'm not staring at a bare URL. I can tag things and actually find them later with a proper search. I can set some aside as "read this later" and clear them out once I've read them. And when I go back to something I saved a while ago, it's still there and still makes sense — even if the original page has changed or disappeared.
>
> Basically, saving should be effortless and finding things again should be reliable. That's the core of what's frustrating today and what should feel different.

> Yes, that's the right direction. You've captured it well.
>
> One thing I'd add so it's clear: when I say "organize," I mean more than just tagging. I want to be able to sort and filter my list, and eventually save the searches I use often so I can pull them up again quickly. And the "put things aside" idea has a couple of sides to it — setting something aside to read later, but also tucking away things I'm done with so they're out of my everyday list without being deleted for good. Deleting should be its own separate, permanent thing.
>
> But the heart of it is exactly what you said. Go with that.

> A web app, please. I'd like to open it in my browser on my computer. It's just for me — I don't need other people logging in or their own accounts. As long as it works comfortably in the browser, that's what I have in mind.
