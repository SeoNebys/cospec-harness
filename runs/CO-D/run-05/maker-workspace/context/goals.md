# Business goal

## North star (in the client's words)

> "Drop a link and trust I'll find it again later."

One place to save a link and **completely trust it can be found again later**, with
**almost no effort at the moment of saving**. The core problem is not storage — it is
*trust*. Today links are scattered across browser bookmarks, texts-to-self, and a
graveyard of open tabs, driven by the fear: "if I let go of this, I'll lose it."

## What "working well" means (client's own criteria)

1. **Effortless saving** — paste a link and it figures out the title / what the page is
   on its own, so the list reads like real titles instead of "a wall of blue web
   addresses."
2. **Loose grouping via labels, not folders** — buckets like "recipes," "work stuff,"
   "articles to read." A single link can carry *several* labels at once and show up
   under any of them. The client explicitly does not want to pick one home per link.
3. **Search that actually works** — type a half-remembered word and find the link.
4. **A "read later" pile** — mark things to read, view just that pile on its own, and
   check them off once read.

## Day-to-day picture (verbatim)

> "paste, it sorts itself out, and later I either search or click into a group and go."

Fast: paste → it sorts itself out → later, search or open a group and go.

## Interface form

- **Home base:** a website opened in a browser on a laptop (where the client reads and
  encounters links during the day).
- **No installed app** — "just something I can pull up in a browser."
- **Phone access:** a nice-to-have, explicitly *not* the main thing. Recorded in the
  non-functional backlog; laptop-in-browser experience is locked down first.

## Verbatim client quotes

- "some just open in tabs forever because I'm scared I'll lose them if I close them."
- "I want one place where I save a link and actually *trust* I can find it again later."
- "so my list is readable at a glance instead of just a wall of blue web addresses."
- "I'd want to stick a couple of labels on something and have it show up under any of them."
- "I'm not really picturing a separate app I have to install — just something I can pull
  up in a browser."
