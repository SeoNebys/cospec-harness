Why old city trees outlive the streets around them

A field report on roots, stonework, and the people caring for ancient urban trees.

By Elena Marin · Originally published 8 September 2026
Saved image · Plane trees along an old stone wall

At the center of the oldest square, the paving bends around a plane tree whose roots began spreading before the surrounding streets carried cars.

The tree survives because generations of caretakers learned to treat the street and the roots as one shared structure, adjusting stonework instead of cutting away new growth.

A record held in wood

Each repair left a trace. Old photographs, growth rings, and city ledgers together tell a story that the modern street alone no longer shows.

Preserving these trees means preserving that record—and making room for living things in places that otherwise seem finished.
