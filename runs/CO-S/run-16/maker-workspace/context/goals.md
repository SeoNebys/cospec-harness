# Cycle 1 goal

## Client statements (verbatim)

> Honestly, right now my bookmarks are a mess. I save links in my browser and they just pile up in a big list — I can't really find anything later, and I forget why I even saved half of them.
>
> What I'm hoping for is a place where saving a link actually captures what it is — like the page's title and a little preview — instead of just a raw web address I don't recognize a month later. Then I want to be able to organize them with my own labels or tags, and actually *search* through them and find the one I need quickly.
>
> A couple of other things that would really help me: being able to mark some links as "read this later" so I can come back to them, and being able to tuck away old ones I'm done with without deleting them forever. And I'd like to jot a note to myself on a link now and then.
>
> Basically, I want my saved links to be something I actually come back to and use, not a junk drawer I never open again.

> Yes, that's exactly the direction. You've captured what matters most to me — saved links that stay recognizable and are easy to find again, so the collection is actually useful.
>
> The only thing I'd add is that finding things is really the heart of it for me: I want the search to be genuinely good, and I want my tags to help me narrow down to just what I'm looking for. As long as we keep that front and center, this is the right path.

## Confirmed goal statement

Build a place where saved links stay recognizable and are easy to find again, so the collection is actually useful and becomes something the client comes back to rather than a junk drawer. Finding things is the heart of the app: search should be genuinely good, and the client's own tags should help narrow the collection to just what they are looking for. Saved links should capture what the page is, such as its title and a little preview, and support reading later, tucking away old links without deleting them forever, and occasional personal notes.

## Interface form

> A web app would be ideal — something I can open in my browser on my computer. That way I don't have to install anything, and it fits naturally with saving links while I'm already browsing the web. Just for me, my own personal collection.

Confirmed as a personal web app used in a desktop browser.
