# Business goal statement

## Cycle 1

### Core goal (in the client's words)

> A single place where I can dump any interesting link and reliably get it back
> later — instead of watching it disappear into a pile I can't search.

The pain is not saving links (that already works). The pain is everything
*after* saving: links vanish into a pile, can't be found again, and "meant to
read" items are lost.

### What must be true for it to feel solved

1. **Findability** — search or narrow to a topic and land on the wanted link,
   without endless scrolling.
2. **Status** — tell "still need to read" apart from "already dealt with," so
   unread stuff doesn't blur into everything.
3. **Recognizability** — a saved link shows what the page actually *is* (its
   name, maybe a short description), not a raw URL.

### Priority / framing (client verbatim intent)

- **Library first.** The quiet measure of success is a *reliable personal
  library where I can find any old link on demand*. "That's the foundation. If
  that's not there, nothing else matters."
- **Read-later as a lens, not a mode.** The "meant to read" pile is a real
  everyday thing. Not a nagging workflow — "one view sitting on top of the same
  library" that can be switched into to work through unread items and mark them
  off. "library first, with the read-later pile as a lens I can switch into
  whenever I want."

### Interface form (confirmed)

- **Web app**, opened in a browser on the client's computer. "That's where I
  live" — the save-a-link moment happens while reading/browsing on the computer.
- **Single personal user.** No accounts, no sharing, no multi-user concerns.
- Deliberately **not** covering phones/other platforms — depth over breadth on
  the one place: finding, searching, read-later pile.

Status: confirmed by client.
