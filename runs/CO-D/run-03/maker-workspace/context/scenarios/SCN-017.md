## Approved behaviour
Layered on top of the everyday "type a few loose words" default, without
disturbing it:

- Exact phrase: wrapping words in double quotes matches those words together, in
  that order.
- Either-word: the plain word "or" between terms ("coffee or tea") matches items
  containing either.
- Exclude: the plain words "not" / "without" (also "except") in front of a word
  leave it out; a leading minus sign ("-word") is kept as an optional shortcut.
- Implicit AND: a bare "and" between words simply means "both", which is already
  the default, so it needs no special handling.
- Literal little words: to search for "or"/"and"/"not" as actual words, wrap in
  quotes ("or") — the SAME quotes rule as exact phrase, nothing new to learn.

A plain-language help line sits under the search box, and a "Reading your search
as:" bar reflects the interpretation back in plain words (exact phrase / either /
without) so the features are discoverable and trusted, not hidden syntax.

## Gherkin
Feature: Deliberate search operators

  Scenario: Exact phrase
    When the client wraps words in double quotes
    Then only items containing those words together in that order match

  Scenario: Either-word
    When the client joins two words with "or"
    Then items containing either word match

  Scenario: Exclude with natural words or minus
    When the client precedes a word with "not", "without", or "-"
    Then items containing that word are excluded

  Scenario: Literal operator word via quotes
    When the client wraps a word like "or" in quotes
    Then it is searched as a literal word, not treated as an operator

  Scenario: Interpretation is shown
    When any operator is used
    Then a plain-language bar shows how the search was interpreted

## Assumptions
- Quotes = phrase AND the literal-word escape (one rule). (Approved.)
- "not"/"without" natural exclusion; minus kept as shortcut. (Approved.)
- Interpretation bar + help line kept prominent. (Approved — key to trust.)
