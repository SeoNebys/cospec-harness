NNATIONAL GEOGRAPHIC•Animals

The surprising intelligence of octopuses

From solving puzzles to using tools, octopuses reveal a remarkable intelligence unlike our own.

By Mara Ellis • Published September 18, 2026
✦Saved image from the original page

Inside a quiet marine lab, an octopus reaches through a narrow opening and turns a transparent box until the lid comes free. The reward is small, but the behavior is striking: the animal remembers what worked before and adjusts when the puzzle changes.

Researchers have watched octopuses use coconut shells as shelter, distinguish individual people, and explore unfamiliar objects with curiosity. Their intelligence evolved along a path very different from our own, distributed through a nervous system that extends deep into each arm.

A mind built differently

That difference is what makes these animals so valuable to study. They offer a reminder that complex problem-solving does not have a single shape—and that a mind can make sense of the world in ways we are only beginning to recognize.
